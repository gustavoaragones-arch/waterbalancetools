Pool chlorine levels chart

What are ideal pool levels?

Ideal pool levels are:
Chlorine: 1–3 ppm
pH: 7.2–7.6
Alkalinity: 80–120 ppm

Direct answer: Daily maintenance for most pools is about 1–3 ppm free chlorine. Temporary shock or algae treatment can require much higher ppm for a short time—always use testing, circulation, and label directions.

Reading the numbers

Test strips and drops report free chlorine—that is what protects bathers. If algae appears or water turns dull, the problem is often filtration, CYA, or pH as much as “more chlorine.” Pair this chart with a shock calculator when you need a large raise instead of guessing bag counts.

After any shock, plan on retesting every few hours while the pump runs until free chlorine returns to a swim-safe range and clarity improves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What chlorine level is safe to swim?

Many pools swim when free chlorine is in the 1–3 ppm range, water is clear, and combined chlorine is controlled—follow product labels and local guidance.

How high should chlorine be when shocking?

Shock treatments often target a temporary raise (for example roughly 10–20 ppm granular equivalent) depending on algae severity—always follow label limits and retest.

Does CYA change chlorine targets?

Cyanuric acid protects chlorine from sunlight but also changes how much measured chlorine is immediately active. Test CYA alongside free chlorine.

Why does my pool smell like chlorine with low readings?

A strong smell often indicates chloramines (combined chlorine) and insufficient sanitizer for bather load—oxidize and test, do not only add more blindly.
