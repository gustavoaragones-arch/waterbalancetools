Steps

  • Test current pH and total alkalinity.
  • Add pH increaser in small doses with circulation.
  • Wait 30–60 minutes; retest pH and adjust again if needed.
  • Target stable pH balance in the recommended range.

What This Means

Moving from 7 to 7.4 is a 0.4-unit change — large single-dose corrections often overshoot, since total alkalinity buffers pH and water needs time to mix. That is why this page emphasizes small steps and retesting. Full explanation: pH guide.

Recommended Levels

  • pH: 7.2–7.6 Recommended
  • Total alkalinity: 80–120 ppm (typical)
  • Free chlorine: 1–3 ppm (pools)

What Happens If Levels Are Off

Low pH increases corrosion risk for metal fixtures and surfaces and can irritate skin/eyes even when sanitizer looks fine. See <a href="../../guides/ph-guide.html">the pH guide</a> for the full picture.

Common Questions

How to adjust pool pH from 7 to 7.4?

That's a 0.4-unit change, using pH increaser (soda ash) — roughly 2.4 oz for a 10,000-gallon pool as a reference point. Add in small increments with the pump running and test after each addition; use the calculator for your exact volume.

Why is pool pH so low in the first place?

Low pH is often caused by heavy rain, certain sanitizers (like trichlor tablets), or high bather load. Check total alkalinity too — very low alkalinity lets pH swing further than expected.

Do I need to fix alkalinity before moving pH from 7 to 7.4?

If total alkalinity is far outside 80-120 ppm, address it first -- alkalinity buffers pH, so a 0.4-unit move will drift back faster (or resist correction) when alkalinity is off. Otherwise, proceed with partial doses and retest after 30-60 minutes.
