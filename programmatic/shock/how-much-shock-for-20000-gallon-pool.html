Steps

  • Test pool volume and current sanitizer level.
  • Pick standard (about 10 ppm raise) or a stronger breakpoint dose (about 30 ppm) to recover from green algae—per label.
  • Broadcast shock with pump running; brush and circulate.
  • Retest before swimming—target safe free chlorine in range.

What This Means

For a 20,000-gallon pool, the ounces on this page scale standard shock math to your exact volume — your starting sanitizer level and how "dirty" the water is still determine whether you need a standard or algae-recovery dose. Full explanation: shock treatment guide.

Recommended Levels

  • After shock: return to 1–3 ppm free chlorine for swimming Typical
  • pH: 7.2–7.6 before/after treatment
  • Total alkalinity: 80–120 ppm (typical)

What Happens If Levels Are Off

Under-shocking can leave the pool sliding back to cloudy or green water; over-shocking keeps swimmers out longer and can stress equipment finishes. See <a href="../../academy/sanitizers/shock-treatments-explained">the shock treatment guide</a> for troubleshooting.

Illustrative scale: about 3.00 units of shock product per 10,000 gallons (example). Real dosing depends on current chlorine and water quality.

Shock dosage reference

Standard shock raises chlorine by about 10 ppm. Green algae recovery calls for a much stronger breakpoint dose. Always verify with testing.

Common Questions

How much shock for a 20,000 gallon pool?

A standard shock (about a 10 ppm chlorine raise) for a 20,000-gallon pool is roughly 20.0 oz of granular shock -- select the 10 ppm preset on the Pool Shock Calculator for a dose specific to your product. Recovering from a green algae bloom calls for a much stronger breakpoint dose, about 30 ppm (roughly 60.0 oz, or 3.75 lb, for 65% calcium hypochlorite) -- that is above the calculator's highest preset (20 ppm), so treat this as a reference figure and confirm against your product's label.

Is a 20,000 gallon pool considered small, medium, or large?

At 20,000 gallons, this is a typical mid-size residential pool. The shock dose scales with volume; the target ppm raise (10 ppm standard, 30 ppm for green algae recovery) stays the same regardless of pool size.

How many bags of shock is that for 20,000 gallons?

Granular shock is commonly sold in 1 lb bags, so a standard dose is roughly 1.3 bags and a green-algae-recovery dose is roughly 3.75 bags at 20,000 gallons -- check your specific product's label, since concentration varies by brand.
