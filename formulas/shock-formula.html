Pool Shock Formula

FormulaExampleExplanation

The Formula

Shock dose (oz) = (Target FC − Current FC) × Volume (gal) × 0.013344 ÷ Available Chlorine (%)
SymbolDescriptionUnit
Target FCTarget free chlorine level. For breakpoint chlorination: 10× combined chlorine. For maintenance: 10 ppm. For algae: 30 ppm.ppm
Current FCCurrent free chlorine in ppmppm
VolumePool volume in gallonsgallons
Available Cl%Available chlorine % of the shock product (e.g. 65 for cal-hypo, 56 for dichlor)%
0.013344Fluid ounces of 100%-available-chlorine product needed per ppm per gallon, the same mass-balance constant used for liquid chlorine (see Phase 7S liquid-chlorine audit and Phase 7T formula-03 audit)constant

Worked Example

Example

Pool: 20,000 gallons. Current FC: 1 ppm. Combined Chlorine: 0.5 ppm. Breakpoint target = 10 × 0.5 = 5 ppm. Using 65% cal-hypo.

  • Target FC = 5 ppm
  • Increase needed = 5 − 1 = 4 ppm
  • Dose = 4 ppm × 20,000 gal × 0.013344 ÷ 65 = 1,067.5 ÷ 65 = 16.4 fl oz (dry weight) = 1.03 lbs

You need approximately 16.4 oz (about 1 lb) of 65% calcium hypochlorite.

How This Formula Works

Shock treatment raises free chlorine quickly to a high level to eliminate combined chlorine (CC), algae, or pathogens. The required dose depends on the shock product's concentration, the pool volume, and how high the FC must be raised.

  • Breakpoint chlorination requires raising FC to at least 10× the combined chlorine level.
  • Maintenance shock raises FC to 10 ppm as a routine preventive treatment.
  • Algae recovery typically requires 20–30 ppm FC.
  • Calcium hypochlorite (cal-hypo) comes in 65% and 73% available chlorine strengths.
  • The dosing math is identical in kind to liquid chlorine (formula-02) — the same mass of 100%-available chlorine is needed per ppm per gallon regardless of whether the product is liquid or dry; only the available-chlorine percentage changes. At 65% cal-hypo this works out to about 2.05 oz per 10,000 gallons per 1 ppm, consistent with the Pool & Hot Tub Alliance's own "Water Chemistry Adjustment Guide" (2 oz per 10,000 gal per 1 ppm for 67% cal-hypo) and this site's own dosage-matrices dataset (2.0 oz for 65%, 1.8 oz for 73%).

Limitations & Notes

Calcium hypochlorite increases calcium hardness — significant doses can raise hardness noticeably, especially in smaller pools or spas. Using cal-hypo repeatedly in a pool with already-high calcium can contribute to scaling. Consider sodium hypochlorite (liquid chlorine) for routine shocking in pools with high calcium hardness. The 0.013344 constant assumes 8.34 lb/gal water density and treats the product's labeled available-chlorine percentage as active-ingredient-by-weight; PHTA's own Calcium Hypochlorite fact sheet notes exact dosage depends on the specific product and directs users to the product label for precise amounts — treat this as a starting dose, not an exact figure.
